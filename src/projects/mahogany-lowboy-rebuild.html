Case study · Moving damage · Structural rebuild

The rebuilt lowboy.

It arrived as a puzzle: one side smashed through, all four cabriole legs snapped off, the case lying in pieces. Moving damage is its own species — not a slow stain but a sudden break — and it has its own order of operations at the bench.

The judgment

Sound first, beautiful second.

A smashed case is a carpentry problem before it is a finishing problem. Glue a broken piece back together in the wrong order and the finish will spend fifty years pointing at the mistake.

So the lowboy was rebuilt the slow way: the case squared and made structurally sound, the four snapped legs re-set so the piece stands as its maker intended, and only then the mahogany refinished. The carved shell drawer and the Queen Anne lines ended exactly where they belong — which is the whole point of a rebuild.

When a customer’s piece comes off a truck broken, this is what a moving or relocation company needs: the piece made right, not merely paid off — with photographs at every stage for the claim file.
