Case study · Private residential · Washington, DC

The Federal Style Collection.

Family heirlooms, passed down for a family member’s new Washington, DC apartment — the finish worn completely through in places, the veneer and inlay beautiful but thin, fragile, and missing in spots. A collection that needed extra care from the first day, and got it.

The judgment

Fragile is a plan, not a problem.

From the start, we knew these pieces needed extra care. Thin veneer decides the method: everything by hand, nothing hurried, no shortcut that could lift an edge that can never be replaced.

After hand stripping, cleaning and sanding, the grain finally showed its depth — the moment every heirloom project turns. From a few stain options applied to the piece itself, the client chose a mahogany finish that brought out the contrasting tones in the grain.

Sealer went on and was sanded down to a smooth, even surface. Then three coats of a high-quality, non-yellowing lacquer — chosen because these pieces are going to be used, not displayed — gave the collection the protection it needs for the next generation.

The result

The collection went home to its new apartment protected for daily life — the family record intact, and legible again.

What this case shows best is the shop’s standard sequence for heirlooms: read the piece, strip by hand because the veneer allows nothing else, let the owner choose color on the actual wood, and build protection in coats rather than shortcuts.
