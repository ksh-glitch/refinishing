02 — The ArrangementHow an account runs

The working terms

Built for more than one piece.

A commercial account is logistics as much as craft: many pieces, a working building, and a schedule that can’t stop for the furniture.

Send photographs of the pieces, or have us walk the property, and the estimate comes back itemized by piece, so you can phase the work as budget and operations allow. We collect and return with our own pick-up and delivery, batch by batch, and every piece is photographed at every stage, so the file shows exactly what was done.

Touch-up and repair are accounts too, not just full restoration — often the difference between a tired floor of guest rooms and a fresh one is a week of disciplined bench work.

  1. Photographs, or a walk-through

    Send pictures of the pieces and the rooms, or arrange a visit. Multi-piece estimates come back itemized, so the account can be phased.

  2. Batched pick-up & return

    We collect and deliver ourselves, batch by batch, scheduled around the building’s operations — the floor keeps working while its furniture is away.

  3. Shop or site

    Pieces travel to Sterling for full restoration; touch-up, repair and fixed woodwork are handled on site when that serves the account better.

  4. Documented throughout

    Damage found, work done, and finished condition — photographed at every stage, for facilities files, owners and claims alike.

04 — QuestionsAsked by accounts

Straight answers

What facilities managers ask.

Can you work around a building that stays open?

That is the normal case. We collect and return in batches with our own van, scheduled around the building's operations, so a floor keeps working while its furniture is away. Touch-up, repair and fixed woodwork are handled on site when that suits the account better.

How are multi-piece estimates presented?

Itemized by piece, so the work can be phased as budget and operations allow. Send photographs of the pieces and rooms, or have us walk the property.

Do you handle touch-up and repair, or only full restoration?

Both. Touch-up and repair are accounts in their own right — often the difference between a tired floor of guest rooms and a fresh one is a week of disciplined bench work rather than a full refinish.

What documentation do we get?

Every piece is photographed at every stage: the damage as found, the work in progress and the finished condition. The same file serves facilities records, owners and insurance claims alike.
