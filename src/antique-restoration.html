Antique restoration · DC · MD · VA

Restored, and still itself.

Antique furniture restoration for Washington DC, Maryland and Virginia: re-gluing and structural rebuilding, veneer and marquetry repair, and period-appropriate refinishing — every step by hand, so the piece keeps its character and its worth. And when a rare piece is better served by conservation than restoration, we say so before any work begins.

01 — The JudgmentRestore · Conserve · Leave alone

Before any stripper is opened

First, decide what the piece deserves.

Restoration is a craft decision and a value decision, and the second comes first. Every antique that reaches this bench is read before it is touched: construction, surfaces, history, and what the piece is worth — at auction and at the dinner table.

For most furniture, careful hand restoration recovers what the maker intended. For a genuinely rare piece with its original finish intact, the honest answer is often to do less — conservation rather than refinishing: gentle cleaning, stabilizing loose veneer, feeding the finish that is already there. We are restorers with a conservation-minded practice, not accredited conservators — and when a piece needs one, we will tell you that too.

The full reasoning is in our guide, Is it worth restoring? — written for owners who want to decide with clear eyes.

02 — The RepairsStructure · Veneer · Color

What the bench does

Repair that protects the value.

An antique is only restored if it is still, recognizably, itself. Our repairs use period-appropriate techniques so the piece keeps its tone, and its worth.

  • Re-gluing loose joints, frames and moldings — taken apart properly, old glue cleaned off, clamped square
  • Structural rebuilding — smashed cases, snapped legs and failed casework made sound again
  • Veneer, inlay and marquetry — loose sections steamed back down, missing pieces replaced and matched
  • Replacement of missing or broken parts, carved to match the original
  • Color and stain matching on the piece itself — you choose on the stripped wood, sample by sample
  • Hardware, keyholes and moldings rebuilt or sourced

The old finish, when it must come off, comes off by hand — never acids, never high-pressure water, never dipping tanks — so fragile veneer, inlay and carving survive the trip to bare wood.
