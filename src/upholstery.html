01 — The WorkroomServing the DMV

Two crafts, one roof

The frame comes first.

Most upholstery shops send the woodwork out; most refinishers send the fabric out. In our Sterling workroom the same small, handpicked team does both, so a loose joint is re-glued, a cracked leg rebuilt and the show-wood hand polished before any new fabric goes on.

Upholstery here goes deeper than fabric. The old cover comes off entirely, the foam is reinforced or replaced with premium materials, and everything underneath — webbing, padding, structure — is rebuilt, so the piece is comfortable and stays that way.

Whether the piece is a family heirloom or simply due for a new life in a new room, the choices stay yours — fabric, detail, finish. No two projects ask for the same work, so timelines vary; we would rather say so than rush.

02 — Fabric & DetailCloth · Leather · Cane

The covering

Your vision, our hands.

Bring the fabric you have fallen for, or let us help you source one suited to the piece and the room. From the old cover coming off to the last trim going on, the materials underneath are chosen to outlast the fabric that covers them.

The traditional details are done the traditional way, by hand: brass nailhead trim set one tack at a time, button tufting pulled through and tied off, damask matched at the seams, leather cut and fitted to carved frames. Where a seat or back was originally caned, it is re-caned, not boarded over.

  • Fresh start — the old fabric comes off carefully, down to the frame, so nothing tired is buried under the new work.
  • New comfort — foam and padding are reinforced or replaced, so the seat is as comfortable in ten years as on the day it comes home.
  • Beneath the cloth — every element underneath is rebuilt with new materials, made to outlast the fabric that covers them.

Fabric change, leather change, full reupholstery of Victorian and century-old pieces — each receives the same focused attention from a team deliberately kept small.

04 — QuestionsAsked at the bench

Straight answers

What clients ask about upholstery.

Can I supply my own fabric?

Yes — client-supplied material is welcome. Send or ship it to the workshop at 45945 Trefoil Lane, Suite 172, Sterling VA 20166, sidemarked with your name or project, and we will confirm the yardage against the piece before anything is cut. If you would rather we sourced it, we can help choose a cloth suited to the piece and the room.

Do you repair the frame as well as the upholstery?

Yes, at the same bench. Most upholstery shops send woodwork out and most refinishers send fabric out; here a loose joint is re-glued, a cracked leg rebuilt and the show-wood hand polished before any new fabric goes on.

Is my sofa worth reupholstering, or should I buy new?

It depends on the frame. A hardwood frame with real joinery is usually worth recovering and will outlast most new retail furniture; a frame of particleboard and staples is not, and we will say so before you spend anything. Send photographs of the whole piece and the underside and we will tell you which you have.

Do you re-cane seats and backs?

Yes. Where a seat or back was originally caned it is re-caned by hand, not boarded over — the weave is part of what the piece is.
